1. The story in a nutshell

Conor O'Malley is a thirteen-year-old boy whose mother is seriously ill with cancer. He is frightened, lonely and struggling to understand what is happening to his family.

At seven minutes past midnight, an ancient yew tree outside his home becomes a monster and visits him. The monster says it will tell Conor three stories. After those stories, Conor must tell a fourth story: the truth about his recurring nightmare.

The novel explores grief, fear, guilt, love, friendship and accepting difficult truths. The monster is not simply there to frighten Conor; it pushes him to face feelings he has been hiding.

Main message: People can have mixed feelings, even when they love someone. Being frightened, angry or confused does not make someone a bad person. Facing the truth can be painful, but it can help people begin to accept what is happening.

3. The monster's stories explained

The three stories are not random. Each one teaches Conor something about people and connects to his own life.

First tale — The Queen and the Prince

Lesson: People are not always what they seem. Do not judge a person only by rumours or appearances. The story challenges simple ideas of heroes and villains.

Second tale — The Apothecary and the Parson

Lesson: Actions have consequences. The parson rejects the apothecary, then later needs his help. The tale explores hypocrisy and the consequences of choices.

Third tale — The Invisible Man

Lesson: Being ignored can be painful. The invisible man wants people to notice him. This connects to Conor's loneliness and his feeling that nobody sees what he is going through.

The fourth tale — Conor's truth

Unlike the first three, this is Conor's own story. He admits that part of him wants the painful waiting and fear to end. He feels guilty because he loves his mother. The monster helps him understand that complicated feelings are human and do not cancel out his love.

4. Important characters

Conor O'Malley

The main character, aged thirteen. He is dealing with his mother's illness, bullying, loneliness, anger and guilt. He learns to face the truth about his feelings.

The Monster

An ancient creature connected to the yew tree. It tells Conor three stories and pushes him to tell the truth. It represents the need to face difficult feelings.

Conor's mother (Lizzie)

She is seriously ill with cancer and loves Conor. Her illness is the main reason Conor is frightened and struggling emotionally.

Grandmother

Strict and organised. Conor initially dislikes her, but she wants to help protect him and care for his mother. They gradually begin to understand one another.

Lily

Conor's childhood friend. Their friendship becomes strained after she tells others about his mother's illness. She later tries to apologise and reconnect.

Harry

A boy at Conor's school who bullies him. His treatment of Conor changes, and he is involved in an important turning point when Conor attacks him.

Conor's father

He lives in America and has another family. He visits Conor, but cannot provide the simple solution or constant support that Conor hopes for.

LoveConor and his mother love each other deeply, even during difficult moments.

TruthThe monster encourages Conor to be honest about his real feelings.

FamilyConor's relationships with his grandmother and father show different kinds of family support.

7. Important events in order

Use this timeline to remember how the story develops from beginning to end.

  1. Conor has a recurring nightmare about his mother.
  2. The yew-tree monster appears at 12:07.
  3. The monster says it will tell three stories and Conor must tell the fourth.
  4. Conor struggles with bullying at school.
  5. His friendship with Lily becomes strained.
  6. His grandmother becomes more involved in caring for the family.
  7. The monster tells the first tale about a queen and a prince.
  8. Conor's father visits from America but cannot give him the support he hopes for.
  9. The monster tells the second tale about an apothecary and a parson.
  10. Conor becomes involved in the destruction of his grandmother's sitting room.
  11. The monster tells the third tale about an invisible man.
  12. Conor attacks Harry at school.
  13. Lily tries to reconnect with Conor.
  14. Conor becomes more aware that his mother's condition is not improving as he hoped.
  15. Conor faces the nightmare and tells the monster the truth.
  16. He begins to understand that mixed feelings do not make him a bad person.
  17. Conor and his grandmother begin to understand each other better.
  18. Conor stays close to his mother and begins to accept what is happening.
